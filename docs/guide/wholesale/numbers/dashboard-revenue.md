# Wholesale numbers: The Revenue tab

This page explains how the Revenue tab is worked out, and why.

## Revenue tab

Two readings, on purpose:

- **Orders total** (the headline figures) follows **orders placed in the period** (not cancelled): **Orders total** =
  Σ order totals; **Received** = payments on those orders dated on or before the period's end; **Left to collect** =
  orders total − received. So *orders total = received + left to collect*. The ranking lists (top customers (8), top products (8) and
  product groups, with share % = part ÷ orders total × 100) are **by what was ordered**. A headline sentence
  names a customer or group that is 40% or more of it.
- **Delivered revenue** is counted **on delivery**: Σ over deliveries in the period (cancelled orders left out) of
  pairs × the order line's price. An order placed but not delivered adds nothing to it. It is what the profit figures on
  the Cost tab are built on.
- **Left to collect** at the end of the period, for the "customers who owe the most" list = Σ over orders dated up to
  the end (cancelled ones left out) of **total − payments dated on or before the end**, when that is above 0. **Delivery
  plays no part**: an order delivered in full and still unpaid counts, the same as on the Customers page and Today's
  Collect list.
- **Potential revenue by day** is a chart of the orders placed each day, this period against the period before, drawn
  only up to today.
- **Open part (no period):** open orders: count, total worth, **Received**, **Left to collect**; how old what is left to
  collect is (the age bands are on the page about periods and open work); the customers who owe the most (top 8, with their orders and the oldest age in days).

## Why it is done this way

- **Two readings, on purpose.** Orders placed measure demand: how much customers ordered. Delivered revenue measures what has really
  gone out. The headline figures on the Revenue tab follow orders placed, and the profit figures on the Cost tab are built on delivered
  revenue, so each figure says which one it is.
- **Orders total equals received plus left to collect.** The three headline numbers always add up.
- **Left to collect ignores delivery.** An order delivered in full and not yet paid is still owed, so it counts. This is the same
  figure the Customers page, Today and the Dashboard show.
- **A headline sentence names a customer or group that is 40% or more.** A business that depends on one customer should see that
  at a glance.
